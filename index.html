What is Cyber Security?

Cyber security means protecting computers, phones, networks and data from people who want to steal or damage them. We use the internet for banking, studying, shopping and chatting, so it affects everyone and not just IT people.

On this website you will learn the basic ideas, the most common attacks, how to protect yourself, and what jobs are available in this field.

Main areas of cyber security

  • Network security - protecting the connections between devices
  • Application security - making apps and websites safe from attacks
  • Endpoint security - protecting laptops, phones and servers
  • Cloud security - protecting data stored on cloud services
  • Information security - protecting data itself
  • Operational security - the rules and processes people follow

Basic security principles

  1. Least privilege - give people only the access they really need
  2. Defense in depth - use many layers of protection, not just one
  3. Zero trust - never trust anyone automatically, always check
  4. Separation of duties - one person should not control everything

The CIA Triad

Almost every security rule is based on these three goals.

Confidentiality
Only the right people can see the data. Example: your WhatsApp chats are encrypted.
Integrity
Data is not changed by someone without permission. Example: your bank balance should not change by itself.
Availability
You can use the system when you need it. Example: a website should not crash because of a DDoS attack.

Types of hackers

  • White hat - hacks with permission to find problems and fix them
  • Black hat - hacks to steal or cause damage
  • Grey hat - in between, no permission but usually no bad intention
  • Script kiddie - uses tools made by others without understanding them
  • Hacktivist - hacks to support a political or social cause
  • State-sponsored - works for a government, usually very skilled

Common Cyber Threats

Malware

Malware is any software made to harm a device or steal information.

Type What it does
Virus Attaches itself to files and spreads when you open them
Worm Spreads through networks on its own
Trojan Looks like a useful program but is harmful
Ransomware Locks your files and asks for money
Spyware Secretly watches what you do
Keylogger Records everything you type, including passwords
Adware Shows unwanted ads and may track you
Rootkit Hides deep in the system so it is hard to find
Botnet A group of infected devices controlled by one attacker

Social engineering

These attacks trick people instead of breaking computers. People are often the easiest target.

  • Phishing - fake emails or websites that steal information
  • Spear phishing - phishing aimed at one specific person
  • Whaling - phishing aimed at top managers
  • Vishing - fraud over phone calls
  • Smishing - fraud through SMS
  • Pretexting - making up a false story to get information
  • Baiting - leaving an infected pen drive for someone to find
  • Tailgating - following someone into a restricted room

How to spot a phishing message

  1. It says something is urgent
  2. The sender's email address looks strange
  3. It asks for a password, OTP or card number
  4. The link goes to a different website
  5. There are spelling mistakes

Network attacks

  • DDoS - sends huge traffic to a website so it stops working
  • Man in the middle - attacker secretly listens to two people communicating
  • DNS spoofing - sends you to a fake website
  • ARP poisoning - tricks devices on a local network
  • Session hijacking - takes over your logged-in session
  • Packet sniffing - captures network data that is not encrypted

Web application attacks

Attack What it means Prevention
SQL injection Attacker types code into a form to attack the database Use parameterized queries
XSS Attacker runs a harmful script in another user's browser Validate input and encode output
CSRF Tricks a logged-in user into doing an action they did not want Use anti-CSRF tokens
Broken authentication Weak login system or weak session handling Use strong passwords and MFA
Clickjacking Hides a button under another button Block the site from being framed

The full list of the most serious web risks is called the OWASP Top 10.

Password attacks

  • Brute force - trying many passwords until one works
  • Dictionary attack - trying common words and passwords
  • Credential stuffing - using leaked passwords on other websites
  • Password spraying - trying one common password on many accounts

Insider threats

Not all attacks come from outside. An employee can leak data on purpose (malicious insider) or by mistake, for example by sending a file to the wrong person (accidental insider).

Advanced threats

  • APT (Advanced Persistent Threat) - a skilled group that stays hidden inside a network for months
  • Supply chain attack - attacking a company by first hacking its supplier
  • Zero-day exploit - attack using a bug that has no fix yet

Mobile threats

  • Fake apps that steal data
  • SIM swapping, where a criminal gets a copy of your SIM
  • Juice jacking, where public USB charging ports are used to steal data

How to Stay Safe

Personal safety checklist

  • Use a different strong password for each account
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)
  • Keep your phone and laptop updated
  • Do not click on unknown links
  • Never tell anyone your OTP or PIN
  • Be careful on public Wi-Fi
  • Take regular backups
  • Lock your phone and laptop with a PIN or fingerprint
  • Download apps only from official stores
  • Check app permissions before you allow them

What makes a good password?

Weak password Better password Why
password123 BlueTrain-Coffee-Window-42 A long passphrase is hard to guess but easy to remember
myname2005 A random password from a password manager Personal details are easy for attackers to guess
Same password everywhere A different password for each site One leak will not affect your other accounts

Authentication methods

  • Something you know - password or PIN
  • Something you have - phone, OTP app or security key
  • Something you are - fingerprint or face

Using two or more of these together is called multi-factor authentication (MFA).

Tools that help

Tool Use
Firewall Blocks unwanted network traffic
Antivirus / EDR Finds and removes malware
VPN Encrypts your internet connection
Password manager Stores strong passwords safely
Disk encryption Protects your files if your laptop is stolen
Patch management Keeps software updated so known bugs are fixed

3-2-1 backup rule

Keep 3 copies of your data, on 2 different types of storage, with 1 copy kept somewhere else (like cloud or another location).

Email security

Companies protect their email using three checks:

  • SPF - lists which servers are allowed to send email for a domain
  • DKIM - adds a digital signature to prove the email was not changed
  • DMARC - tells receivers what to do if SPF or DKIM fails

Network Security

A network is where computers talk to each other, so it needs protection too.

Firewall
Works like a security guard that allows or blocks traffic based on rules.
IDS and IPS
An Intrusion Detection System warns you about suspicious activity. An Intrusion Prevention System also blocks it.
Network segmentation
Dividing a network into parts so an attacker cannot reach everything if one part is hacked.
VPN
Creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and another network.
Wi-Fi security
Use WPA2 or WPA3 and a strong Wi-Fi password. Never use open networks without protection, and change the default router password.

Common ports

Port Service Note
21 FTP File transfer, not encrypted
22 SSH Secure remote login
25 SMTP Sending email
53 DNS Converts website names to IP addresses
80 HTTP Normal websites, not encrypted
443 HTTPS Secure websites
3389 RDP Windows remote desktop, often attacked

Cloud security

Cloud providers like AWS, Azure and Google Cloud follow a shared responsibility model. The provider secures the data centre and hardware, but the customer must secure their own data, accounts and settings. Most cloud breaches happen because of wrong settings, like leaving a storage bucket public.

Cryptography

Cryptography is the science of hiding information so only the right person can read it. Normal text is called plaintext and the scrambled version is called ciphertext.

Type How it works Example
Symmetric Same key to lock and unlock AES
Asymmetric Public key to lock, private key to unlock RSA, ECC
Hashing One-way, cannot be reversed SHA-256

Caesar cipher example

The Caesar cipher is an old method where each letter is shifted by a fixed number. With a shift of 3, A becomes D, B becomes E and so on. So the word CAT becomes FDW. It is easy to break, but it is good for understanding the idea.

How HTTPS works (simple version)

  1. Your browser asks the website for its certificate
  2. The browser checks that the certificate is valid and trusted
  3. Both sides agree on a secret session key
  4. After that, all data is encrypted using that key

When you see the padlock and https in your browser, it means the connection between you and the website is encrypted.

More topics

  • Salting - adding random data to a password before hashing it
  • Digital signature - proves who sent a message and that it was not changed
  • Digital certificate - proves a website is really who it says it is
  • End-to-end encryption - only the sender and receiver can read the message
  • Steganography - hiding a secret message inside an image or file
  • Post-quantum cryptography - new methods designed to stay safe from future quantum computers

Ethical Hacking

Ethical hackers test systems with written permission to find weak points before criminals do. Hacking without permission is illegal.

Phases of ethical hacking

  1. Reconnaissance - collecting information about the target
  2. Scanning - looking for open ports and weak points
  3. Gaining access - using a weakness to get in
  4. Maintaining access - checking if the attacker could stay inside
  5. Reporting - writing down what was found and how to fix it

Types of testing

Black box
The tester knows nothing about the system.
White box
The tester has full information, even the source code.
Grey box
The tester has some information.

Teams

  • Red team - acts like the attacker
  • Blue team - defends the system
  • Purple team - red and blue teams working together

Popular tools (for learning and authorised testing)

Tool What it does
Nmap Scans a network to find devices and open ports
Wireshark Captures and shows network traffic
Burp Suite Tests web applications for weaknesses
Metasploit A framework used in penetration testing

Bug bounty

Many companies pay people who find and report security bugs responsibly. Platforms like HackerOne and Bugcrowd list these programs.

Security Operations and Incident Response

What is a SOC?

A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a team that watches a company's systems all day and night. They use tools like a SIEM (for example Splunk or Wazuh) that collect logs and raise alerts when something looks wrong.

Incident response steps

  1. Preparation - make a plan and train the team
  2. Identification - find out that an attack is happening
  3. Containment - stop it from spreading
  4. Eradication - remove the cause, like malware
  5. Recovery - bring systems back to normal
  6. Lessons learned - study what went wrong so it does not happen again

Digital forensics

Digital forensics means collecting and studying digital evidence from computers and phones. The evidence must be handled carefully, and a record called the chain of custody shows who handled it and when, so it can be accepted in court.

Disaster recovery

Companies also make a business continuity plan and a disaster recovery plan, so they can keep working or recover quickly after an attack, fire or power failure.

Laws, Rules and Frameworks

Risk management

Risk is how likely a threat is to use a weakness, and how bad the damage would be. Companies check their risks, decide which ones to reduce, and write security policies for employees to follow.

Frameworks

  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework - guidelines to identify, protect, detect, respond and recover
  • ISO 27001 - international standard for managing information security
  • CIS Controls - a list of practical security actions
  • MITRE ATT&CK - a knowledge base of how attackers behave

Laws and regulations

Law or standard Region About
IT Act, 2000 India Main law for cyber crime and electronic records
DPDP Act, 2023 India Digital Personal Data Protection law
CERT-In India National agency that handles cyber incidents and issues guidelines
GDPR European Union Data privacy law
HIPAA USA Protects health information
PCI-DSS Worldwide Security standard for companies that handle card payments

Privacy and security

Security protects data from attackers. Privacy decides how personal data is collected and used. You need both: a company can keep data very secure and still misuse it.

New and Special Areas

IoT security

Smart cameras, smart TVs, watches and home devices are all connected to the internet. Many have weak default passwords, so attackers use them to build botnets. Always change default passwords and update the device.

Industrial and OT security

Power plants, factories, water supply and railways use control systems like SCADA. An attack on them can affect the real world, so they need strong protection.

AI and cyber security

  • Defenders use AI to detect unusual behaviour and block attacks faster
  • Attackers use AI to write more believable phishing emails
  • Deepfakes are fake videos or voices that look real and can be used for fraud
  • Prompt injection is when someone gives hidden instructions to an AI chatbot to make it behave wrongly

Other emerging threats

  • Cryptojacking - secretly using your device to mine cryptocurrency
  • QR code phishing - fake QR codes that lead to harmful websites
  • Cyber warfare - attacks between countries on important infrastructure

The dark web

The dark web is a hidden part of the internet that needs special software like Tor to open. It has some legal uses, like private communication, but stolen data is also sold there. Security teams watch it to see if their company data has leaked.

Cyber Safety in India

Common frauds

  • OTP fraud - a fake bank caller asks for your OTP
  • UPI scam - you are asked to enter your PIN to "receive" money (you never need a PIN to receive money)
  • Fake job offers - they ask you to pay a registration fee
  • KYC scam - a message says your account will be blocked unless you update KYC
  • Lottery scam - you "won" a prize but must pay first
  • Fake customer care - scammers put fake helpline numbers online

If you become a victim

  1. Call your bank right away and block your card or account
  2. Call the cyber crime helpline 1930
  3. Report on cybercrime.gov.in
  4. Change your passwords
  5. Keep screenshots and messages as proof

What to do if your account is hacked

  1. Disconnect the device from the internet
  2. Change passwords from another safe device
  3. Turn on 2FA
  4. Scan the device for malware
  5. Tell your friends if your social media was used to send spam

Safe use of social media

  • Keep your profile private if you can
  • Do not post your phone number, address or travel plans
  • Do not accept friend requests from strangers
  • Check your privacy settings regularly

For Kids, Parents and Small Businesses

Tips for kids

  • Never share your password with anyone except your parents
  • Do not talk to strangers online
  • Do not share photos or your school name publicly
  • Tell a trusted adult if something online makes you uncomfortable

Tips for parents

  • Talk with children about what they do online
  • Use parental controls and safe search
  • Keep devices in common areas of the house
  • Teach children that it is okay to say no and to ask for help

Tips for small businesses

  • Use MFA on email, banking and accounting accounts
  • Keep regular backups and test them
  • Train employees to spot phishing
  • Update software and the router
  • Give employees only the access they need

Glossary

Attack surface
All the places where an attacker could try to get in.
Authentication
Proving who you are, for example with a password.
Authorization
Deciding what you are allowed to do after you log in.
Encryption
Turning data into a secret code.
Exploit
A way to use a weakness to attack a system.
Patch
An update that fixes a security bug.
Payload
The harmful part of malware.
Risk
The chance of something bad happening, and how bad it would be.
Threat
Anything that could cause harm.
Vulnerability
A weakness in a system.
Zero-day
A bug that the maker does not know about yet, so there is no fix.

FAQ

Is a VPN enough to keep me safe?

No. A VPN protects your connection, but it does not stop phishing, malware or weak passwords.

Are password managers safe?

Good ones encrypt your passwords. Using one is safer than reusing the same password.

Do Macs and iPhones get viruses?

Yes, they can. They are not immune, so updates and safe habits are still important.

How can I check if my email was in a data breach?

Go to Have I Been Pwned and enter your email.

Do I need a degree to start a career in cyber security?

Not always. Skills, certifications and hands-on practice matter a lot, but many people also start with a computer science degree.
